# MFA and Authentication

## Authentication factors
- Something you know: password or PIN
- Something you have: hardware security key or authenticator device
- Something you are: biometric characteristic

Multi-factor authentication combines at least two different factor categories. Two passwords are not two different factors.

## Stronger choices
Phishing-resistant passkeys and FIDO2 security keys are generally stronger against credential phishing than SMS codes. Use unique passwords and a reputable password manager as well.

## Common risks
- Password reuse and credential stuffing
- MFA fatigue or push-bombing
- SIM swapping against SMS-based recovery
- Weak account recovery procedures

## Defensive checklist
- Enable MFA, prioritizing email and administrator accounts.
- Prefer phishing-resistant methods where supported.
- Review recovery options and sign-in alerts.
- Never approve an unexpected authentication prompt.

## Key takeaway
MFA reduces risk, but secure recovery and user awareness remain important.
